I am good at making planning systems more elaborate. Keeping them useful is harder. The weekly review I want is short enough to do on an ordinary Friday, including the Fridays when I would rather close the laptop.

Lately, I have been thinking about the review as a small handoff between this week and the next one.

## Look back before planning

An unfinished list tells only part of the story. Before moving tasks forward, I look for what actually happened: a bug fixed, a difficult decision made, a conversation that changed the direction of a project.

That record does not have to be polished. A few sentences help me distinguish a slow week from a week whose useful work was never on the list.

> What moved forward, even if I did not plan it?

## Make three decisions

Carrying every unfinished task into the next week makes the list heavier without making it clearer. I try to decide what still deserves attention.

- Keep: something that still matters next week.
- Change: something that needs a smaller or clearer next step.
- Drop: something I no longer intend to do.

Dropping a task can feel surprisingly difficult. Writing down the decision helps. The task is no longer waiting silently; I have chosen to spend that time elsewhere.

## Leave a starting point

I finish by choosing one thing that would make Monday easier. It should be an action I can begin, not a broad ambition like ‘work on the app.’

> [!NOTE] A smaller next step
>
> Instead of ‘improve onboarding,’ try ‘write the first screen someone sees after opening the app.’

The ritual works best when it leaves me with less to hold in my head. A short review, a few deliberate choices, and a starting point are usually enough.
